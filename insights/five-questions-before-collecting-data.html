Five questions to settle before a programme starts collecting data

Monitoring and evaluation often begins with a list of indicators. A short set of decisions about what the data is for makes a stronger start. These five questions are a useful test of any results framework before the first survey goes out.

1. What decision will this information change?

Tie every indicator to a decision someone has to make: continue, adjust, scale up or stop. Indicators with no decision attached add cost and are rarely read.

2. What is the result, in one sentence?

If the intended change cannot be stated plainly, the framework will drift towards counting activity. Outputs record what the programme did. Outcomes describe what changed for people or institutions because of it. A framework needs both, and needs to keep them apart.

3. Where does the baseline come from?

A target means little without a starting point. Settle early whether the baseline will come from existing administrative data, a new survey or a documented estimate, and record the choice.

4. Who collects the data, and how often?

Reporting that depends on frontline staff with full workloads tends to arrive late or incomplete. Match the frequency of collection to the pace at which the result can change, and give the task to people who have time for it.

5. When will the team stop and learn?

Schedule review points at which the data is examined and the plan can change. Learning that waits for the final evaluation arrives too late to help the programme it describes.

A framework that answers all five questions is usually shorter than its first draft, and more likely to be used.
